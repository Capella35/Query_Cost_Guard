row got the right verdict (6/6), but the judge also rejected all 8 good proposals

**Cause (found in the reasons, not guessed).** Every G-row reason leads with
`recent_median_sec`: "recent runs are well above the baseline, so the new
threshold would kill current legitimate runs." By design the Investigator only
proposes after a regression: the last 5 runs' median is ≥ 1.5× the baseline
median (`investigator.REGRESSION_RATIO`), and the new threshold is meant to catch
that kind of slowdown. The harness builds every proposal the same way
(`recent_median` = baseline median × 1.6–3.0). The judge's prompt never says
what `recent_median_sec` is or why it is high, so the judge reads the regression
as the new normal, and on that reading rejecting is reasonable.

**Classification** (CLAUDE.md "When the Guard and the policy disagree"): not a
model failure. **Missing context** in what the judge is told, on top of an open
**design question**: is a 5-run regression a runaway to stop, or the new normal
(data growth, S2/S6) to let through? The payload cannot tell them apart.

**Reasons on the subtle rows.** Each cites its own signal (S1/S5 bimodal tail,
S2/S6 trend ratio 1.708, S3 the one-hour span, S4 the threshold at p95), but
most lead with the same recent-median argument that rejected the good rows.
So 6/6 is partly the right verdict for a reason that does not tell good from
bad.
